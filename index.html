The goal

Performance marketing is a small world. The brand CMOs and publisher leads you're trying to reach follow the same feeds and sign with networks whose names they recognize. There are already voices in this category who've built that kind of standing through consistent LinkedIn presence. None of them run a network at FlexOffers' scale.

GA
Gal Aga Co-Founder & CEO at Aligned · 92,000 followers 3w

I have to come clean. LinkedIn is not for all CEOs. It drives ±60% of our ARR. We get 50M+ views/year across internal + Partner/UGC posts. But in Q1, I stopped posting regularly, and leads from LinkedIn dropped 50%.

We're still growing fast, but navigating this was painful.

I've talked a lot about the upside of founder-led growth.

But this is the part we don't talk about enough.

Founder-led growth has a cost.

And most founders underestimate it.

DON'T invest in LinkedIn if you:

1. CAN'T COMMIT TO THE TIME TAX

Writing isn't the hard part. The context switching is. Whenever I post, it steals focus for 60-90min mid-workday. I make final edits, reply to comments, and engage in other posts. Add that to 6-9hr writing/week... Big commitment.

2. DON'T HAVE THE MENTAL BANDWIDTH

You're never "done." Every walk, dinner, or meeting becomes a potential post. The effort isn't the hard part. Staying inspired and excited to write is. And when you stop publishing for a while, restarting feels heavier than starting from zero.

3. CAN'T COMMIT TO CONSISTENCY (FIRST MONTHS, THEN YEARS)

This one hurts most. Momentum is fragile. Miss a few weeks, and the drop is steeper than you expect. In Q1, I posted 50% less. Almost 50% was old content. And I almost didn't engage. My reach crashed, and we had to compensate for it.

4. DON'T TRULY ENJOY BUILDING RELATIONSHIPS (CAN'T SKIP THIS)

Posting is the easy part. What breaks people is everything around it. The replies. The DMs. Showing up in others' feeds. Skip it and your audience feels it immediately. They stop coming back. And so do the leads.

5. NEED CLEAN ATTRIBUTION AND TIDY ROI SPREADSHEETS

It won't show up as last-touch. ~60% of our ARR self-report LinkedIn - but you can't prove the full impact in the CRM right away. Your board will ask. You won't have a clean answer. If you need the dashboard before you commit, you'll quit before it works.

This isn't ads you can treat like a transaction you just pump $$ into.

And it's not PR where you just optimize for the message.

It's trust, built in public.

That takes time and attention.

For me, that's 10+ hours/week.

And having to continuously "growth hack" when things break.

Founder-led growth works.

But it has a cost - and we're not talking about it enough.

Questions buyers actually ask

What you're probably wondering. Plain answers.

I get it's important. But why not wait a quarter?+
The LLM citation race doesn't pause for Q3. Every week without exec content is a week LinkedIn's index builds around your competitors' posts, and that's the exact source ChatGPT and Perplexity pull from when a brand asks which affiliate network to trust. The compounding curve on LinkedIn takes 90 days to start showing. A Q3 start means a Q4 lift at best, and Q1 data for the brand-deal attribution conversations you actually need. Starting now means real numbers before fall budget planning season. Section 03 has the specific breakdown on what one quarter of delay costs you.
Why not just have ChatGPT or Claude do this?+
You can. Most LinkedIn content already does. Originality.ai's 2025 study of 3,368 posts from 99 influential profiles flagged 53.7% of long-form LinkedIn content as likely AI-generated. That's exactly the problem. The reason posts hitting your feed feel forgettable is that they all read the same. We use AI heavily for the unsexy work: scraping competitor posts, tracking what category leaders publish, keeping your context graph current. Real humans on our team write every post. The system gives us category fluency on day one; the humans give you something the algorithm hasn't already seen 10,000 times this week.
FlexOffers works with publishers and advertisers. Are either of them on LinkedIn?+
Both sides of your marketplace are active on LinkedIn. Publishers are content creators and digital marketers: LinkedIn is their primary professional network for tracking industry moves and finding programs worth promoting. Advertisers are brand and performance marketing leaders who read LinkedIn daily to evaluate partners. There's no audience-fit concern here. Your buyers are already on the platform. The gap is simply that you're not speaking to them there yet.
What if Geoff doesn't want to post, or hates the drafts?+
Nothing ships without Geoff's approval. The five-minute Slack review is a feature, not a workaround. If a draft isn't right, we rewrite. Most agencies sell engagement metrics. We measure ICP-relevant audience growth. We start from a Context Graph built from Geoff's actual post history, voice fingerprint, and the specific angles FlexOffers already owns. The cadence is the product, not the volume.
